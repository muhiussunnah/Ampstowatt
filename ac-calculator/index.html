What Is a BTU?

A British Thermal Unit (BTU) is a heat energy unit used to rate heating and cooling capacity. One BTU is about 1,055 joules, 252 calories, or 0.293 watt-hours. In air conditioning, BTU per hour measures how much heat an air conditioner can remove from indoor air.

1 BTU=0.293 Whand1 W=3.412 BTU/h
Size

Size and Ceiling Height

Size and ceiling height control the air volume that needs cooling or heating. Larger rooms and high-ceiling spaces need more BTU capacity. A room with 500 square feet and an 8 foot ceiling needs less cooling than the same room with a 14 foot ceiling.

R

Insulation Condition

Insulation condition changes BTU demand by changing heat gain and heat loss. Good insulation lowers cooling load during heatwave events and lowers heating load during winter. Poor insulation, single-pane windows, attic heat, and air leakage raise BTU requirements.

Temp

Desired Temperature Increase or Decrease

Desired temperature increase or decrease is the difference between outdoor conditions and the target indoor temperature. A hot room at 95 F with a 75 F target needs a 20 F temperature decrease. A cold room at 40 F with a 70 F target needs a 30 F temperature increase.

Def

Definition of the Term Wattage

Wattage is the amount of electric power required to run an electrical appliance. The unit of measurement is watts with the abbreviation W. A 25 W light uses 0.025 kilowatts in one hour of operation, which is 0.025 kilowatt-hours of electrical energy.

AC

What Does AC Mean and Where Do We Use It?

Alternating Current (AC) is electric current that reverses direction with a regular frequency. Homes, schools, businesses, HVAC systems, Central Air Conditioning, Heat Pumps, and many motors use AC. Direct Current (DC) flows in one direction and is common in batteries, electronics, and solar storage.

Need

What Do You Need to Know to Calculate AC Wattage?

To calculate AC wattage, you need voltage, current, power factor, and phase type. Three-phase calculations need line-to-line or line-to-neutral voltage type. The calculator, tool, machine, or instrument may use a display screen, keypad, battery, circuit board, processor, and power button, like a Texas Instruments TI-84, Casio fx-991EX, HP Prime, or Sharp EL-W516T.

1P

How to Calculate Wattage From Volts and Amps Using AC Single Phase Current

To calculate wattage from volts and amps using AC single phase current, multiply power factor by current and voltage. The formula is P = PF x I x U, where P is power in watts, PF is power factor, I is current in amperes, and U is voltage in volts.

P=PFxIxU

Example: 24 volts, 3.75 amperes, and PF 0.5 gives P = 0.5 x 3.75 x 24 = 45 W.

LL

How to Calculate Wattage Using AC Three Phase Current for Line-to-Line Voltage

To calculate wattage using AC three phase current for line-to-line voltage, multiply the square root of 3 by power factor, current, and line-to-line voltage. The formula is P = 1.732 x PF x I x V. Example: 120 volts, 5 amperes, and PF 0.8 gives P = 1.732 x 0.8 x 5 x 120 = 831.36 W.

LN

How to Calculate Wattage Using AC Three Phase Current for Line-to-Neutral Voltage

To calculate wattage using AC three phase current for line-to-neutral voltage, multiply 3 by power factor, current, and line-to-neutral voltage. The formula is P = 3 x PF x I x V. Example: 12 volts, 0.125 amperes, and PF 0.2 gives P = 3 x 0.2 x 0.125 x 12 = 0.9 W.
