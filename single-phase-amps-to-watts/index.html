AC Single Phase Amps to Watts Calculator

An AC single phase amps to watts calculator uses W = A x V x PF. Amps are the current, volts are the supply voltage, and PF is the power factor. A resistive load with PF 1.00 uses the simple formula watts = amps x volts. An inductive load such as a single phase motor, transformer, or compressor needs the power factor included.

Example: 15A at a 120V supply and PF 1.00 equals 1,800W. The same 15A at 240V and PF 0.85 equals 3,060W. This single phase amps to watts calculator helps with single phase power, Alternating Current (AC), electrical load, apparent power, real power, wattage rating, ampere rating, and load calculation. Use the result for planning, then verify wire size, circuit breaker, electrical panel, voltage drop, and equipment nameplate data.

A 120V supply needs about double the current of a 240V supply for the same wattage. Higher current increases voltage drop, breaker load, and wiring heat. For motor load work, compare the calculated current with Full Load Amps (FLA) and motor inrush current.

1 Amp to Watts

1 amp equals watts equal to volts times power factor in single phase AC. At 120V and PF 1.00, 1A equals 120W. At 220V and PF 1.00, 1A equals 220W. At 240V and PF 1.00, 1A equals 240W. At 240V and PF 0.80, 1A equals 192W.

This conversion is useful for amp calculator watts volts, amps volts watts calculator, and electrical calculator watts amps volts searches. Direct Current (DC) uses watts = amps x volts with no power factor. Alternating Current (AC) single phase loads can require power factor when real power and apparent power differ. Apparent power is volts x amps. Real power is apparent power x power factor.

Watts to Amps Single Phase

Watts to amps single phase conversion uses A = W / (V x PF). This formula turns wattage rating into current. Use it for appliance load, single phase motor watts calculator, transformer load, and circuit breaker planning. A 3,600W resistive load at 240V draws 15A. The difference matters for single phase electrical load calculator work. Resistive load examples include heaters and load banks. Single Phase Motor Amps and Starting Current

Single phase amps are calculated from the load wattage, supply voltage, and power factor. For example, 5,000W at 240V and PF 1.00 is 20.83A. At PF 0.85, the current is 24.51A. A single phase motor can draw much more during starting than during normal running.

3 Phase to Single Phase Amps Calculator

A 3 phase to single phase amps calculator compares equivalent real power, not just amps. First calculate three phase watts with W = 1.732 x V x A x PF. Then calculate single phase amps with A = W / (V x PF). This gives a practical comparison when replacing or comparing equipment across phase types.

Example: 20A at 400V three phase and PF 0.90 equals 12,470W. On 240V single phase at PF 0.90, the same real power would draw about 57.73A. This is why three phase power can carry larger electrical loads with lower current per conductor. Check transformer capacity, electrical panel limits, circuit breaker rating, and wiring before changing phase type.

Calculate Single Phase Watts from Apparent Power

Calculate single phase watts from amps and volts by multiplying amps x volts x power factor. A 12A load on 120V at PF 1.00 is 1,440W. A 12A motor on 120V at PF 0.80 is 1,152W of real power and 1,440VA of apparent power.

This distinction matters for single phase electrical power conversion. Watts describe real power and energy consumption. Volt-amperes describe apparent power and equipment loading. Kilowatts show real power at a larger scale. Kilovolt-ampere (kVA) helps size transformers, inverters, and generators. Use both values when the load has a power factor below 1.00.

FAQ

Single Phase FAQ

Fast answers before you use the calculator or compare formulas.

W How do I convert AC amps to watts at 230V?

For single-phase AC: Watts = Amps × 230 × Power Factor. For pure resistive loads (PF = 1.0), it simplifies to Watts = Amps × 230. Example: 10A at 230V with PF 0.90 = 2070 watts real power.

VA What is the difference between real watts and apparent power (VA)?

Real watts (W) represent usable power that does actual work — heat, light, motion. Apparent power (VA) is the total current × voltage drawn from the supply, including reactive current that does no useful work. The ratio is power factor: Watts = VA × PF. A 1,000 VA UPS at PF 0.80 delivers only 800W of real power.

PF Why are my AC watts lower than amps × volts?

Because inductive loads (motors, compressors, transformers) draw reactive current that oscillates between the source and load without doing useful work. This lowers the power factor below 1.00, so real watts are less than the simple amps × volts product. Only resistive loads (heaters, incandescent lamps) give watts equal to amps × volts.

MTR How do I measure power factor?

You can measure PF with a power quality meter or clamp meter that reads both watts and VA simultaneously. PF = Watts ÷ VA. Alternatively, check the equipment nameplate — most motors, compressors, and industrial equipment list PF at rated load. If unknown, 0.85 is a conservative estimate for motor loads.

HOME Can I just ignore power factor for household circuits?

For most residential calculations, yes. Household resistive loads (lights, heaters, toasters) have PF very close to 1.00. But for whole-house load calculations that include HVAC compressors, well pumps, and refrigerators, ignoring PF will overestimate real power by 10–20%. For generator sizing, always account for PF.

CAL Does this calculator work for both 230V and other voltages?

Yes — you can change the voltage field to any value. The calculator applies the correct formula for DC, AC single-phase, or AC three-phase regardless of voltage. This page defaults to 230V for convenience, but the formula works for 12V, 24V, 48V, 208V, 480V, or any other voltage.
