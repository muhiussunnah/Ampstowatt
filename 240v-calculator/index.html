240V Power Calculation Formula

The 240V power calculation formula is P = 240 x I x PF for AC loads. Use PF 1 for resistive heaters, electric ranges, and many heating elements. Use the equipment power factor for motors, compressors, transformers, and variable frequency drive harmonic filtering.

Power=240 VxAmpsxPF
VAI

Voltage Current Watt Relationship

The voltage current watt relationship states that watts equal volts times amps. Ohm's Law links voltage, current, resistance, and power. For 240V systems, 15 A equals 3,600 W at PF 1, and 40 A equals 9,600 W at PF 1.

AC

AC Power Calculation Examples

AC power calculation examples at 240V need PF for real power. A 30 A load at PF 1 equals 7.2 kW. A 30 A load at PF 0.8 equals 5.76 kW real power and 7.2 kVA apparent power.

P

Electrical Load Calculations at 240V P=V x I

Electrical load calculations at 240V use P=V x I for PF 1 loads. Add each running load, convert watts to kW, then compare total load against the panel, circuit breaker, wire size, voltage drop, derating factor, and National Electrical Code (NEC) requirements.

1P

Single Phase 240V Power Formula

The single phase 240V power formula is watts = 240 x amps x PF. For PF 1 loads, 16A equals 3.84 kW, 30A equals 7.2 kW, 32A equals 7.68 kW, and 40A equals 9.6 kW.

VA

Real Power and Apparent Power Calculations

Real power is measured in watts or kW, and apparent power is measured in VA or kVA. A 240V, 30 A circuit has 7.2 kVA of apparent power. At PF 0.8, real power equals 5.76 kW.

PF

Power Factor Calculations

Power factor calculations convert apparent power into real power. Use kW = kVA x PF. A 9.6 kVA load at PF 0.8 delivers 7.68 kW of real power. Poor PF raises current for the same useful output.

kWh

Electrical Energy Consumption Examples

Electrical energy consumption equals kW multiplied by hours. A 3.6 kW load running for 2 hours uses 7.2 kWh. A 7.2 kW load running for 4 hours uses 28.8 kWh. A 9.6 kW load running for 30 minutes uses 4.8 kWh.

Hm

Household Appliance Wattage Examples

Household appliance wattage examples at 240V include EV chargers, electric showers, ranges, dryers, water heaters, heat pumps, and welders. A Small EV Charger at 16A is about 3.6 kW, a Standard EV Charger at 32A is about 7.2 kW to 7.68 kW depending on current limit, and an Electric Shower at 40A is about 9.6 kW.

A

Convert Watts Into Amps at 240V

To convert watts into amps at 240V, divide watts by 240 and PF. The formula is amps = watts / (240 x PF). For PF 1 loads, 1 kW equals 4.17 A, 2 kW equals 8.33 A, and 10 kW equals 41.67 A.

Draw

Current Draw Examples

Current draw examples show the difference between PF 1.0 and PF 0.8. A 5 kW load draws 20.83 A at PF 1.0 and 26.04 A at PF 0.8. A 15 kW load draws 62.50 A at PF 1.0 and 78.13 A at PF 0.8.

CB

Circuit Breaker Load Calculations

Circuit breaker load calculations compare expected current with breaker rating, continuous load rules, wire size, and terminal rating. Include breaker sizing, continuous load, non continuous load, conductor sizing, distance factor, voltage loss, ambient temperature, conduit fill, arc fault breaker derating factor, and overcurrent protection.

I

Appliance Current Demand Examples I = P / V

Appliance current demand examples use I = P / V for PF 1 loads. A 3.6 kW Small EV Charger draws 15.00 A. A 7.2 kW Standard EV Charger draws 30.00 A. A 9.6 kW Electric Shower draws 40.00 A. A 20 kW load draws 83.33 A at PF 1 and 104.17 A at PF 0.8.
