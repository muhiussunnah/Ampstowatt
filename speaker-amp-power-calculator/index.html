Amplifier supply path

Audio output watts translated into DC supply current

Amplifier pages need efficiency and fuse context, because speaker watts and supply current are not the same measurement.

Formula Amps = Watts / (Volts x Efficiency)

Quick answer

Use this calculator to estimate amplifier supply current, fuse size, and power draw.

Speaker output watts are not the same as supply watts. Efficiency, voltage, impedance, and amplifier class change the current the electrical system must provide.

Amps = Watts / (Volts x Efficiency)

Inputs to check before calculating

  • Use RMS amplifier power rather than peak marketing watts.
  • Enter supply voltage from the actual battery or power supply.
  • Use realistic amplifier efficiency for Class AB or Class D systems.
  • Check speaker impedance so the amplifier is operating in its rated range.

Example: 1,200 watts at 12 volts

At 12 volts, a 1,200 watt load draws 100 amps before AC power-factor adjustments. Use the power factor field when the equipment is a motor, compressor, inverter, or other inductive load.

Best use cases

  • Car audio friendly
  • Efficiency input
  • Fuse planning context
  • DC load planning with clear unit labels.

Common mistake to avoid

Do not assume a 500W audio amplifier draws only 500 / 12 amps; efficiency loss and voltage sag increase current.

How to use the result

Use the current estimate for fuse planning, cable sizing, and battery load checks.

Accuracy and safety note

Calculator output is a planning estimate. Final breaker sizing, wire gauge, derating, voltage-drop limits, equipment protection, and code compliance should be verified with manufacturer documentation and a qualified electrician or engineer.

Speaker Amplifier Power Calculator

A speaker amplifier power calculator estimates amplifier watts, speaker load, supply current, and power consumption for a speaker system. The calculator uses amplifier output watts, speaker impedance in ohms, supply voltage in volts (V), amplifier efficiency, and play time in hours (h).

There are 4 main values to check before matching an amplifier and speaker: amplifier root mean square (RMS) watts, speaker RMS power rating, speaker impedance, and supply current. Peak watts do not size wiring or fuses. RMS watts gives the useful planning value.

Speaker Amp Wattage Calculator

A speaker amp wattage calculator matches amplifier RMS wattage to speaker RMS wattage at the correct impedance. A speaker rated for 100 W RMS at 8 ohms works with an amplifier that delivers about 75 W to 150 W RMS into 8 ohms, when gain is set cleanly.

Use 3 wattage checks: speaker RMS rating, amplifier RMS output at the same impedance, and total channel count. A 4 channel amp rated 75 W x 4 at 4 ohms can power 4 speakers at 75 W RMS each, if each speaker is 4 ohms and each speaker uses one channel.

Subwoofer Amp Power Calculator

A subwoofer amp power calculator sizes amplifier power by subwoofer RMS watts, voice coil wiring, impedance, and supply current. A 600 W RMS subwoofer at 2 ohms needs an amplifier that is stable at 2 ohms and rated near 600 W RMS at 2 ohms.

Subwoofer systems draw more current than door speakers since bass notes need higher amplifier output. A 1000 W subwoofer amplifier on a 12 V car audio system draws about 98 A at 85% efficiency: 1000 / (12 x 0.85) = 98 A.

Amplifier Speaker Wattage Calculator

An amplifier speaker wattage calculator divides amplifier output across connected speakers and checks the impedance load on each channel. One 4 ohm speaker on one channel creates a 4 ohm channel load. Two 4 ohm speakers wired in parallel create a 2 ohm load, and the amplifier must support 2 ohms on that channel.

A 4 channel amp powers 4 speakers when each channel drives one speaker at the rated impedance. A 4 channel amp can power more speakers only when the final impedance per channel stays within the amplifier rating.

dB

Speaker Sensitivity and Amplifier Power Calculator

A speaker sensitivity and amplifier power calculator estimates watts from speaker sensitivity, target loudness, listening distance, and headroom. Speaker sensitivity is usually measured as decibel sound pressure level (dB SPL) at 1 watt (W) and 1 meter (m).

Every 3 dB increase needs about 2 times the amplifier power. Every 10 dB increase needs about 10 times the amplifier power. A speaker rated 90 dB SPL at 1 W / 1 m needs about 10 W to reach 100 dB SPL at 1 m before distance loss and headroom.

How Much Amplifier Power for Speakers

Use amplifier power close to the speaker RMS rating at the same impedance. For normal use, choose an amplifier rated between 75% and 150% of the speaker RMS rating. This range gives headroom without forcing the amplifier into clipping.

There are 5 practical rules: match RMS watts, match impedance, leave headroom, avoid clipping, and size supply wiring for current draw. A small clipped amplifier can damage speakers faster than a larger clean amplifier with controlled gain.

How to Calculate Amp for Speakers

To calculate amp for speakers, divide amplifier watts by supply voltage and amplifier efficiency. The formula is A = W / (V x efficiency). A 300 W amplifier on a 12 V car system at 85% efficiency draws 29.4 A.

NEED

How Much Amplifier Power for One Speaker

For one speaker, use amplifier RMS power near the speaker RMS rating. A 75 W RMS speaker works with about 75 W RMS per channel at the same impedance. For two speakers, calculate each channel separately.

kWh

Calculate Speaker Power Consumption

To calculate speaker power consumption, multiply average amplifier watts by hours, then divide by 1000 for kilowatt-hours (kWh). A system averaging 120 W for 5 hours uses 0.6 kWh: 120 x 5 / 1000 = 0.6 kWh.

HEAD

How Much Amplifier Power for Loud Listening

For loud listening, choose amplifier power with 3 dB to 6 dB of headroom. 3 dB headroom means about 2 times the power above the average listening level. 6 dB headroom means about 4 times the power.

FAQ

Speaker Power FAQ

Fast answers before you use the calculator or compare formulas.

AMP How many amps does a car amplifier draw?

Divide the amplifier RMS output power by (supply voltage × efficiency). A 500W RMS Class AB amp at 13.8V with 50% efficiency draws 500 ÷ (13.8 × 0.50) = 72.5 amps. Class D amps are more efficient (75–90%), drawing significantly less: 500 ÷ (13.8 × 0.85) = 42.6 amps.

FUSE What fuse size do I need for my car audio amplifier?

Calculate maximum current draw from the amplifier total power consumption (not output power). A 500W RMS amp at 50% efficiency consumes 1,000W total. At 13.8V: 1,000 ÷ 13.8 = 72.5A. Add 20% headroom → 87A fuse. Most car audio fuse blocks use ANL or mini-ANL fuses in standard sizes (60A, 80A, 100A).

EFF Why does my amplifier draw more amps than the output watts suggest?

Because amplifier efficiency is never 100%. Class AB amps are typically 50–65% efficient, meaning they consume roughly double the audio output power as total electrical power. The remainder becomes heat. Class D amps are 75–90% efficient, wasting less power as heat.

AWG What wire gauge do I need for a car amplifier power cable?

Match wire gauge to the total current draw and cable length. For 80A at 15 feet (one way), 1/0 AWG or 2 AWG copper is typical. For 40A at 10 feet, 4 AWG is usually adequate. Always include a fuse within 18 inches of the battery terminal. Check voltage drop — car audio is sensitive to voltage sag under load.

Ω How do I convert speaker impedance into amplifier current?

At full power output: Amps = √(Watts ÷ Impedance). A 100W channel into 4 ohms: √(100 ÷ 4) = 5A RMS per channel at the speaker terminals. Supply-side current is higher due to efficiency losses. Note: this is speaker-terminal current, not power supply current.

RMS Peak watts vs RMS watts — which should I use for calculations?

Always use RMS (continuous) watts for current draw and fuse calculations. Peak watts are a marketing figure representing momentary maximum output and are typically 2× RMS. A "1,000W peak" amplifier likely produces 500W RMS. Use the RMS value to avoid undersizing fuses and wiring.
