**H2:** Breaker Safety for 10-Amp Loads

A 10-amp load on a **15A breaker at 120V** = 1,200W, which is 67% of the 1,800W maximum — this is **safe for continuous use**.

A 10-amp load on a **20A breaker at 120V** = 1,200W, which is 50% of the 2,400W maximum — also **safe for continuous use** (NEC requires ≤80%).

For a dedicated 10-amp load, a 15-amp branch circuit at 120V is typically sufficient.

**H2:** Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How many watts is 10 amps at 120V?**
A: 10 amps at 120 volts equals 1,200 watts (10 × 120 = 1,200). This is the wattage of a standard microwave oven or a 1,200W space heater on a US household outlet.

**Q: How many watts is 10 amps at 240V?**
A: 10 amps at 240 volts equals 2,400 watts (10 × 240 = 2,400). This is typical for smaller air conditioning units, water heaters, and some EV charging circuits.

**Q: How many watts is 10 amps at 12V DC?**
A: 10 amps at 12 volts equals 120 watts (10 × 12 = 120). Common for DC motors, LED strip drivers, and automotive accessories.

**Q: Can a 15-amp breaker handle 10 amps continuously?**
A: Yes. The NEC requires continuous loads stay at or below 80% of the breaker rating. 10 amps is 67% of a 15A breaker, which is within safe limits. The 15A breaker's 80% continuous limit is 12 amps.

**Q: What is the formula for 10 amps to watts?**
A: Watts = Amps × Volts. For 10 amps: Watts = 10 × [Voltage]. For AC with power factor: Watts = 10 × Voltage × PF.

**H2:** The Formula: Why Watts Depend on Voltage

Watts per amp isn't a fixed number — it scales directly with voltage because of Watt's Law: P = I × V.

**H3:** Why Isn't There a Fixed "Watts Per Amp" Answer?
Unlike a fixed conversion (like 1 mile = 1.609 km), watts per amp requires voltage as an input. Without voltage, the conversion is incomplete.

**H3:** DC vs AC Watts Per Amp
For DC circuits, watts per amp = voltage (simple multiplication, no power factor).
For AC circuits with inductive loads, real watts per amp = voltage × power factor.

Example: On a 120V AC circuit with a motor at PF 0.85:
Watts per amp = 120 × 0.85 = **102 real watts per amp** (not 120W).
