The Basics of Amps to Watts Conversion

PF

Amps to watts conversion changes electrical current into electrical power by multiplying current by voltage and the correct phase factor. Single alternating current uses watts = volts x amps x power factor. Three phase power uses watts = 1.732 x volts x amps x power factor when voltage is measured phase vs phase.

=

The balanced 3 phase amps to watts formula is watts = 1.732 x line-to-line volts x line amps x power factor. The 1.732 factor comes from the three-phase voltage relationship. Use line-to-line voltage for most 3 phase power calculation work, such as 400V, 415V, or 480V systems.

3 Phase Amps to Watts FAQ
How many watts are in 3 phase power?

3 phase watts equal 1.732 x line-to-line volts x line amps x power factor. At 400V, 16A, and PF 1, the result is about 11,085 W.

How many amps are in 3 phase power?

3 phase amps equal watts divided by 1.732 x volts x power factor. An 11 kW load at 400V and PF 1 draws about 15.88 A.

What is the difference between 1-phase and 3-phase charging?

1-phase charging uses one alternating current at 230V, while 3-phase charging uses three conductors at 400V. 3-phase charging delivers more kW charging power at the same amp rating.

Does a 32A cable force 32A charging?

No, a 32A charging cable does not force 32A charging. The EV, onboard charger, charging station, and grid limit current automatically when the equipment is designed to regulate current.

When should I include power factor?

Include power factor for AC loads when real watts matter. Motors, transformers, UPS systems, variable frequency drives, and non-linear loads often need power factor correction in the calculation.
